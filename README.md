## Part I. Connection to Genome Browser and ClinVar

1. On which chromosome is ATXN3 located?

ATXN3 is located on chromosome 14 (14q32.12).

2. What disease-associated variant did you examine previously?

NM_004993.6(ATXN3):c.916G>C (p.Gly306Arg)

ClinVar Variation ID: 128515

dbSNP: rs12895357

Previously examined in ClinVar. Its GRCh38 genomic position was chr14:92,071,010.

3. In the current Cell Browser dataset, which cell types express ATXN3?

ATXN3 is detected across several cell types, including neuronal populations such as IT-related clusters and Endothelial cells. Some Oligodendrocyte, Astrocyte, and Microglia populations show lower or little detectable expression.

4. Does the observed cell expression make biological sense based on what you know about ATXN3?

Yes. ATXN3 is related to SCA3, a neurological disease, and it is expressed in several cell types in the human cortex. This makes sense because ATXN3 is not limited to only one cell type.

5. Can this single Cell Browser dataset prove that ATXN3 causes SCA3? Why or why not?

No. The Cell Browser only shows where ATXN3 is expressed. It cannot prove that ATXN3 causes SCA3. Other evidence, such as genetic and clinical studies, is needed.

## Part J. Reflection

1. What did the UCSC Cell Browser show you that the UCSC Genome Browser could not?

The UCSC Cell Browser showed me where ATXN3 is expressed in different cell types. The Genome Browser mainly shows the gene's location, structure, and genetic information.


2. Why can the same gene have different expression levels among different cell types?

Different cell types have different functions, so they do not need to express every gene at the same level. This is why ATXN3 can have different expression levels in different cell types.


3. Why should you be careful when interpreting a gene that shows zero or very low expression in single-cell data?

Zero or low expression does not always mean that the gene is completely inactive. It can be affected by the number of cells, the tissue used, and the methods used to collect the data.


4. Why is it useful to combine information about genomic location, genetic variants, and cell-specific gene expression?

Combining these types of information gives a better understanding of how a gene may be related to a disease. For ATXN3, I can connect its chromosome location, disease-associated variant, and expression in different brain cell types.


5. What was the most interesting observation you made about your assigned gene?

The most interesting observation was that ATXN3 was expressed in several different cell types instead of being limited to only one cell type. This helped me understand that a disease-associated gene can have a broad expression pattern.
